# Complete 2D Photodiode Simulation Guide

This guide details the physics, mathematics, and simulation workflow for a 2D p-n junction photodiode, tailored for TCAD tools like DEVSIM, using a mesh generated by the provided `gmesh_diode2d.py` script.

-----

## 1\. Device Structure (2D p-n Junction Photodiode)

The simulation is based on a 2D cross-section of a vertical photodiode. The geometry consists of three distinct semiconductor regions and named boundaries (contacts and surfaces) that are essential for applying boundary conditions.

### 2D Geometry (from `gmesh_diode2d.py`)

```
      Light ↓ (incident on y=0 surface)
      <-- width -->
      +-----------+
      |  cathode  |
+-----+-----------+-----+  y = 0
|     n+ region         |  (n_plus_region)
|-----------------------|  y = n_plus_thickness
|                       |
|       p region        |  (p_region)
|                       |
|-----------------------|  y = n_plus_thickness + p_thickness
|     p+ substrate      |  (p_plus_region)
+-----------------------+  y = total_depth
|         anode         |
+-----------------------+
```

The key geometrical entities defined in the Gmsh script are:

  * **Regions (Surfaces):** `n_plus_region`, `p_region`, `p_plus_region`
  * **Contacts (Curves):** `cathode` (top contact), `anode` (bottom contact)
  * **Surfaces (Curves):** `top_surface`, `left_side`, `right_side`

## 2\. Governing Equations (2D Drift-Diffusion Model)

The behavior of the device is described by a set of coupled partial differential equations solved over the 2D domain $(x, y)$.

### 2.1 Poisson's Equation

This equation relates the electrostatic potential $\psi$ to the local charge density.

**Equation:**
$$\nabla \cdot (\varepsilon \nabla \psi) = -\left( \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\varepsilon \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}\right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(\varepsilon \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}\right) \right) = -q(p - n + N_D^+ - N_A^-)$$

Where:

  * $\psi(x, y)$ = electrostatic potential [V]
  * $\varepsilon$ = material permittivity [F/cm]
  * $q$ = elementary charge [C]
  * $n(x, y), p(x, y)$ = electron and hole concentrations [cm⁻³]
  * $N_D^+, N_A^-$ = ionized donor and acceptor concentrations [cm⁻³]

### 2.2 Electric Field

The electric field $\mathbf{E}$ is a vector field derived from the potential.

**Equation:**
$$\mathbf{E}(x, y) = -\nabla \psi = -\left( \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}\mathbf{\hat{x}} + \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}\mathbf{\hat{y}} \right)$$

### 2.3 Current Densities (Drift-Diffusion)

The electron and hole current densities are vector quantities, each with a drift and a diffusion component.

**Electron current vector:**
$$\mathbf{J}_n = q\mu_n n \mathbf{E} + q D_n \nabla n$$

**Hole current vector:**
$$\mathbf{J}_p = q\mu_p p \mathbf{E} - q D_p \nabla p$$

**Total current vector:**
$$\mathbf{J}_{total} = \mathbf{J}_n + \mathbf{J}_p$$

### 2.4 Einstein Relations

These relations connect the diffusivity ($D$) and mobility ($\mu$).
$$D_n = \mu_n \frac{k_B T}{q} \quad \text{and} \quad D_p = \mu_p \frac{k_B T}{q}$$

### 2.5 Continuity Equations (Steady State)

These equations describe the conservation of charge, balancing current flow with generation and recombination.

**Electrons:**
$$\frac{1}{q}\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}_n + G - U = 0$$

**Holes:**
$$-\frac{1}{q}\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}_p + G - U = 0$$

Where:

  * $G(x, y)$ = optical generation rate [cm⁻³s⁻¹]
  * $U(x, y)$ = total recombination rate [cm⁻³s⁻¹]

-----

## 3\. Recombination Models

These models are identical to the 1D case, as they describe local physical phenomena. The total recombination rate $U_{total}$ is the sum of the individual rates.

  * **Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH):** $U_{SRH} = \frac{np - n_i^2}{\tau_p(n + n_1) + \tau_n(p + p_1)}$
  * **Radiative:** $U_{rad} = B(np - n_i^2)$
  * **Auger:** $U_{Auger} = (C_n n + C_p p)(np - n_i^2)$

-----

## 4\. Optical Generation

For vertical illumination, the generation rate primarily depends on the depth ($y$) into the device.

**Monochromatic Illumination:**
$$G(y, \lambda) = \alpha(\lambda)[1 - R_s(\lambda)]\Phi_0(\lambda)e^{-\alpha(\lambda)y}$$

Where:

  * $y$ = depth from the illuminated surface at $y=0$.
  * $\alpha(\lambda)$ = absorption coefficient [cm⁻¹]
  * $R_s(\lambda)$ = surface reflectance (fraction)
  * $\Phi_0(\lambda)$ = incident photon flux [photons/(cm²·s)]

-----

## 5\. Boundary Conditions for 2D Simulation

Boundary conditions (BCs) are applied to the 1D curves defined in the Gmsh script. Let $\mathbf{\hat{n}}$ be the outward-pointing normal vector for each boundary.

### 5.1 Ohmic Contacts (Dirichlet BCs)

These conditions fix the potential and assume charge neutrality.

  * **`cathode` (top contact):**
      * Applied voltage: $\psi = V_{applied}$
      * Equilibrium carriers: $n = N_D$, $p = n_i^2/N_D$
  * **`anode` (bottom contact):**
      * Ground reference: $\psi = 0$
      * Equilibrium carriers: $p = N_A$, $n = n_i^2/N_A$

### 5.2 Insulating/Passivated Surfaces (Neumann BCs)

These surfaces are assumed to be perfectly insulating (no current flows through them) but can have surface recombination.

  * **Boundaries:** `top_surface`, `left_side`, `right_side`
  * **No normal electric field:** $\nabla \psi \cdot \mathbf{\hat{n}} = 0$
  * **Surface Recombination:** The normal component of the current density is equal to the surface recombination rate.
      * $\mathbf{J}_n \cdot \mathbf{\hat{n}} = q S_n (n - n_{eq})$
      * $\mathbf{J}_p \cdot \mathbf{\hat{n}} = -q S_p (p - p_{eq})$
      * Where $S_n, S_p$ are the surface recombination velocities [cm/s].

## 7\. Performance Metrics

### 7.1 Terminal Current

In 2D, the total current is calculated by integrating the normal component of the total current density vector $\mathbf{J}_{total}$ along the length of a contact.

**Equation:**
$$I(V) = \int_{\text{contact}} \mathbf{J}_{total} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{n}} \, dl$$

Where the integral is performed along the `cathode` or `anode` boundary. The result is in Amperes per unit meter [A/m] for a 2D simulation, representing the current for 1 meter of device length into the page.

### 7.2 Quantum Efficiency & Responsivity

These formulas remain the same, but they use the 2D-calculated photocurrent $I_{ph}$.

**External QE:** $EQE(\lambda) = \frac{I_{ph}(\lambda)/q}{\Phi_{inc}(\lambda) \cdot W}$ (where W is device width)
**Responsivity:** $\mathcal{R}(\lambda) = \frac{I_{ph}(\lambda)}{P_{opt}(\lambda)} = \frac{q\lambda}{hc} \cdot EQE(\lambda)$ [A/W]

## 8\. 2D Solution Workflow

### Step 1: Mesh Generation

1.  **Run the `gmesh_diode2d.py` script.** This creates the `gmsh_diode2d.msh` file.
2.  The script defines the geometry, assigns physical names to regions and boundaries, and applies mesh refinement rules critical for accuracy (e.g., fine mesh at the junction, contacts, and surfaces).

### Step 2: Equilibrium (Dark, V=0)

1.  Import the mesh into the TCAD solver (e.g., DEVSIM).
2.  Solve Poisson's equation over the 2D domain to find the built-in potential $\psi(x, y)$ and equilibrium carrier concentrations $n(x, y)$, $p(x, y)$.

### Step 3: Dark I-V Curve

1.  For each bias voltage $V_{applied}$ on the `cathode`:
      * Solve the fully coupled Poisson and continuity equations with $G=0$.
      * Calculate the terminal current by integrating the current density along the `anode` or `cathode` boundary as described in Section 7.1.

### Step 4: Illuminated Operation

1.  Define the optical generation profile $G(y, \lambda)$.
2.  For a given bias (e.g., V=0 for QE calculation), solve the fully coupled equations with the non-zero $G(y, \lambda)$.
3.  Extract the photocurrent $I_{ph}$ by subtracting the dark current from the total illuminated current.
4.  Repeat for different wavelengths to calculate $EQE(\lambda)$ and Responsivity $\mathcal{R}(\lambda)$.

### Step 5: Generate Plots

1.  **2D Contour Plots:** Visualize variables across the device, such as electrostatic potential $\psi(x, y)$, carrier concentrations $n(x, y)$ and $p(x, y)$, and recombination rates $U(x, y)$.
2.  **2D Vector Plots:** Show the direction and magnitude of the electric field $\mathbf{E}(x, y)$ and current densities $\mathbf{J}_n(x, y)$, $\mathbf{J}_p(x, y)$.
3.  **1D Line Cuts:** Extract data along a specific line (e.g., vertically through the center of the device at $x = \text{width}/2$) to create plots like $n(y)$, $p(y)$, and band diagrams.
4.  **Characteristic Curves:** Plot I-V curves (dark and illuminated) and spectral response (QE vs. λ).

-----

## 9\. 2D Numerical Implementation Notes

  * **Discretization:** The governing equations are typically discretized on an unstructured triangular mesh (as generated by Gmsh) using the Finite Volume Method (FVM), which is well-suited for conservation laws like the continuity equations.
  * **Solver Strategy:** A Gummel or Newton-Raphson iterative method is used to solve the non-linear system of equations at each bias point.
